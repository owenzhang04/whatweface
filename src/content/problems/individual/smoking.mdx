## What it is

Smoking means breathing in smoke from burning tobacco, mostly in cigarettes but also in cigars, pipes, bidis and water pipes. Tobacco damages nearly every organ in the body. It causes cancer, heart disease, stroke and lung disease, and leads to early death.<Cite source="who-tobacco-factsheet" />

Nicotine, the drug in tobacco, is highly addictive, which is why quitting is hard. Smoke also harms the people nearby: there is no safe level of second-hand smoke.<Cite source="who-tobacco-factsheet" />

## How big it is

The Global Burden of Disease study measures how many years of healthy life are lost to each health risk. Smoking accounts for <Stat id="dalys-share" /> of all healthy life lost worldwide, behind only high blood pressure and air pollution. The next few risks, including high blood sugar, are close enough that their order isn't certain. For men, smoking is the biggest risk of all.<Cite source="gbd-2023-risk-factors" />

Men are far more likely to use tobacco than women: <Stat id="men-prevalence" /> of men do, against <Stat id="women-prevalence" /> of women.

## Where it's heading

The harm is falling for each person, but not yet in total. Smoking's share of healthy life lost is about where it was before, at <Stat id="dalys-share-2010" withPeriod />. The number of years lost has barely moved: the estimate changed by <Stat id="dalys-change" />, a range that includes no change at all. For a person of a given age, though, the risk has fallen by <Stat id="rate-fall" />, one of the largest falls among the leading risks. It fell in every region of the world.<Cite source="gbd-2023-risk-factors" />

Fewer people use tobacco. The share of adults who do fell from <Stat id="prevalence-2010" withPeriod /> to <Stat id="prevalence" withPeriod />. Countries agreed to cut tobacco use by <Stat id="target-cut" /> from that earlier level by the middle of this decade. Women reached that goal years early. Men are well behind, and the world as a whole is on course to get there a few years late. In Africa and the Eastern Mediterranean, the number of tobacco users is still rising.<Cite source="who-tobacco-trends-2025" />

## What works

WHO's package of tobacco control measures is called MPOWER: monitor tobacco use, protect people from smoke, offer help to quit, warn about the dangers, enforce bans on advertising, and raise taxes. <Stat id="protected" /> people now live under at least one of these measures at the level WHO recommends. Raising prices through tax is the most effective way to cut tobacco use, but it is the measure that has spread most slowly.<Cite source="who-tobacco-epidemic-2025" />

For people who smoke, quitting is hard, but help works. WHO recommends that health workers give brief advice to quit to every tobacco user they see, with counselling for anyone who wants to quit, and medicines: nicotine replacement therapy (patches, gum or lozenges), varenicline, bupropion and cytisine. Medicines and counselling work best together.<Cite source="who-tobacco-cessation-guideline-2024" />
